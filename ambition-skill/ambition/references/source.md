**Article:** Felix Rieseberg, "How to be more ambitious: A tutorial for humans with models"
<https://felixrieseberg.com/how-to-be-more-ambitious-a-tutorial-for-humans-with-models/>

This skill paraphrases and restructures the article for agent use. It doesn't reproduce the original, so read the article itself for the full argument.

## From the article

- The four types of ambition: speed, altitude, depth, range. Speed is skipped as the uncreative default.
- Altitude: aim at the outcome and ask what should happen next. Meetings are a signal. Open-ended goals ("make money") don't work well yet. Durable workflows need changes to the user's own setup. The crash-free example and the three-rung QBR escalation.
- Depth: work like top professionals until experts could learn from the result. Instruments over vocabulary. Unreasonable goals framed as reasonable. Challenge assumptions. Produce new knowledge validated on unseen data. Use an outside judge. The four-question "deeper, not bigger" rewrite pattern.
- Range: many fields via subagents. Nouns → verbs (credited to Kelley and Littman). Assembling a crew that includes adversaries (short seller, forensic accountant). Assign activities rather than role-play personas.
- Prerequisite: a computer the model can use, with software installation and data access.
- Self-efficacy evidence: Locke and Latham, Collison's "Fast", Dantzig, Bannister, the sub-two-hour marathon, *The Inner Game of Tennis*, Linux "just a hobby", Michelangelo.

## Added by this skill

- The step order, completion criteria and the two modes (advise vs. self-apply). Self-apply includes the guardrail that the user chooses the rung.
- The "ambition ladder" deliverable format (prompt / judge / access / cost per rung) and the "one line on why an axis doesn't apply" exit.
- "Stop one level before abstract" as a working rule for altitude.
- The shallow-vs-deep instrument table, the extra nouns→verbs rows (onboarding flow, bug report), and the crew table format.
- The specific tools in the worked example (DuckDB, Cox model, eight-quarter back-test, 60-day signal). The article describes these methods only in general terms.
- The capability-vs-belief triage in `self-efficacy.md`.
