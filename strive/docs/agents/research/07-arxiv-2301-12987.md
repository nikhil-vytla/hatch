## Source-supported facts

**Framing (abstract, §1).** Generalisation is cast as: given sets A ⊂ B, infer from A a hypothesis sufficient to construct B. The paper attacks the widespread "proxy for intelligence" assumption that compression (choosing the *shortest* hypothesis, per Solomonoff induction / Kolmogorov complexity / MDL) is what makes hypotheses generalise, within a formalism of enactive cognition.

**Formalism (§2–3, Definitions 1–8).** A set Φ of states; declarative programs f: Φ → {true, false} (the set P); a vocabulary 𝔳 is a finite subset of P; statements in the implementable language L_𝔳 are satisfiable subsets l ⊆ 𝔳; the **extension** Z_l of a statement is the set of statements it entails. A 𝔳-**task** α = ⟨S_α, D_α, M_α⟩: situations (inputs/examples), correct decisions (outputs), and **models** — statements l with Z_{S_α} ∩ Z_l = D_α. **Induction** (Def. 8): given proxy q_𝔳, choose hypothesis h ∈ argmax_{m ∈ M_α} q_𝔳(m); generalisation means h ∈ M_ω for the wider task ω ⊐ α. **Weakness** (Def. 7) is the cardinality of the extension |Z_l|; the **description-length** proxy is q_𝔳(l) = 1/|l| (shorter formulae preferred).

**Main results (§4).**
- **Proposition 1 (sufficiency):** weakness maximisation maximises the probability that induction generalises from α to ω; the proof derives p(h ∈ M_ω | h ∈ M_α, α ⊏ ω) = 2^{|Z̄_{S_α} ∩ Z_h|} / 2^{|Z̄_{S_α}|}, maximised by maximising |Z_h|.
- **Proposition 2 (necessity):** to maximise generalisation probability it is *necessary* to use weakness or a function thereof (if |Z_h| < |D_ω|, generalisation is impossible since D_ω ⊄ Z_h).
- **Proposition 3:** description length is *neither necessary nor sufficient* — an explicit small-vocabulary counterexample shows minimising |m| does not maximise |Z_m|.
- **Headline claim (abstract, verified verbatim):** "if tasks are uniformly distributed, then there is no choice of proxy that performs at least as well as weakness maximisation in all tasks while performing strictly better in at least one."
- **Remark 1:** under a uniform task distribution, p(h ∈ M_ω | h ∈ L_𝔳) = 2^{|Z_h|} / 2^{|L_𝔳|} yields a *computable* prior, contrasted with AIXI's incomputable Kolmogorov-complexity prior (Hutter, def. 5.14 cited).

**Experiments (§5).** Φ = 256 states (8-bit strings); tasks are 4-bit binary addition and multiplication (4-bit inputs + output packed into 8 bits, one target bit predicted). A parent task T_n has D_n = all valid results; situations S are decisions with one randomly deleted bit. A child task T_k is sampled uniformly from the parent (|D_k| from 4 to 14 examples); from the child's data two models are built — c_w (maximum weakness) and c_mdl (minimum description length, via SymPy `simplify_logic` CNF minimisation) — then tested for exact reconstruction of the parent's D_n. "Between 75 and 256 trials were run for each value of the parameter |D_k|" (§5.2; fewer for larger |D_k| due to cost). Error bars: Wald 95% CI for generalisation rates, standard error for extent (§5.3).

**Results (§5.3, Tables 1–2).** Weakness beats MDL at every sample size, with the gap widening as evidence grows:
- Addition, generalisation rate (c_w vs c_mdl): |D_k|=6: .11±.039 vs .10±.037; |D_k|=10: .27±.064 vs .13±.048; |D_k|=14: **.68±.106 vs .24±.097**.
- Multiplication: |D_k|=6: .05±.026 vs .01±.011; |D_k|=10: .16±.045 vs .08±.034; |D_k|=14: **.46±.061 vs .21±.050**.
- Summary: weakness generalised at 1.1–5× (110–500%) the rate of MDL; average *extent* of generalisation 103–156% of MDL's.

**Discussion (§6).** Ockham's razor is reinterpreted: "A simple statement need not be weak… a complex utterance can assert nothing. Weakness is a consequence of extension, not form." The paper proposes the alternative razor, verbatim: **"Explanations should be no more specific than necessary."** It attributes the Apperception Engine's generalisation to its universally-quantified (hence weak) rules rather than to its Kantian framing (§6.0.1), and speculates that LLM "fabrication and inconsistency" stems from optimising loss rather than weakness (§6.0.2). Conclusion: the compression↔intelligence link is overstated, at least for enactive cognition under uniform task distributions.

## Analysis dimensions

- **Runtime, task, environment, and state models.** Purely formal: finite state set Φ, tasks as ⟨situations, decisions, models⟩ triples, no runtime or environment. The experiment is an offline enumeration over 8-bit propositional statements. No agent loop of any kind. Largely not addressed by source beyond the task formalism itself.
- **Observation, trajectories, traces, memory, persistence.** Not addressed by source. There are no trajectories or traces; "evidence" is a static example set D_k.
- **Trusted/immutable vs evolvable surfaces.** Not addressed by source. (The proxy q_𝔳 plays the role strive's acceptance rule plays — a fixed selector applied to candidates — but the paper never frames it as a trust boundary.)
- **Recursive decomposition, subagents, context management.** Not addressed by source.
- **Candidate generation and self-modification.** Not addressed as engineering; formally, the candidate set is *all* models of the evidence (M_α), i.e., exhaustive generation, with selection carrying the entire burden. No self-modification.
- **Evaluation, selection, promotion, rollback, lineage.** Selection theory is the paper's entire content: among candidates indistinguishable on visible evidence (all are exact models of D_k), the choice of tie-breaking proxy alone determines held-out generalisation, and the provably optimal proxy is weakness (extension size), not shortness. Nothing on promotion mechanics, rollback, or lineage.
- **Sandboxing, secrets, permissions, budgets, recovery.** Not addressed by source. (Search `depth_limit`/`time_limit` in `experiments.py` are compute caps on the experiment itself, not harness budgets.)
- **Online adaptation vs offline optimization.** Not addressed by source; the setting is one-shot offline induction from a fixed sample.
- **Harness adaptation vs model-weight learning.** Not addressed by source. Neither exists here; the result is architecture-independent theory about hypothesis choice.
- **Genuinely self-improving vs merely persistent/configurable.** Not addressed by source — there is no system to improve. The paper is *about* the selection principle any self-improving system needs.
- **Mechanisms suitable for a robust, long-lived harness.** One, and it is a principle rather than a mechanism: when multiple candidates tie on visible evidence, prefer the one that commits to the least beyond that evidence (maximal extension / minimal specificity), and do not trust shortness/compression as a stand-in. Also transferable: the experimental *protocol* — sample child task from parent, select on child, score exact reconstruction of parent — is a clean template for measuring acceptance-rule generalisation.

## Interpretations

*The following are inferences by this reviewer, not claims of the paper.*

1. **This is a theory of tie-breaking for strive's `decide` stage.** strive's acceptance rule already requires empirical improvement; but whenever two candidates pass the visible suite equally (which becomes common once a model proposer emits multiple patches), the choice among them is exactly the paper's induction problem. The paper says: the deciding proxy should reward *low specificity to the visible cases*, not small diffs.
2. **"Minimal bounded patch" is an MDL-flavoured prior, and the paper argues that razor is wrong for generalisation.** strive v0's registry of minimal patches is justified on *safety/reviewability* grounds (small blast radius), which the paper does not touch; but insofar as anyone expects minimal diffs to *generalise* better, Proposition 3 says that expectation is unfounded. Safety-of-change and generality-of-change are different axes and should be scored separately.
3. **Weakness is why held-out validation works.** Proposition 1's probability calculation is essentially a formal account of D6: a hypothesis overfitted to the visible split (strong, small extension) has provably lower probability of covering held-out decisions. Held-out testing *measures* what weakness *predicts*; strive can use both — weakness-flavoured features as a cheap pre-filter/rank, held-out as the gate.
4. **Reward hacking is strength-seeking.** An eval-overfitting candidate is one that encodes the visible test cases (maximally strong, near-zero extension beyond them — e.g., a lookup table of expected outputs). A weakness-style detector (does the candidate's behavior collapse outside the visible inputs?) is a principled anti-gaming signal, cheaper than a full held-out run.
5. **Direct computation is intractable for strive's surface.** |Z_l| is computable only because the paper's language is tiny finite propositional logic. For arbitrary Python strategies, extension size is undecidable; any use in strive must go through behavioral proxies (probe-input equivalence classes, mutation/perturbation coverage), which reintroduces the proxy-fidelity problem the paper itself warns about (any proxy other than true weakness is dominated).
6. **Scope limits.** All guarantees assume a *uniform distribution over child tasks of a parent* and exhaustive candidate sets; strive's tasks are not uniformly distributed, and its candidate sets are whatever the proposer emits. The experiments are 8-bit toy problems with n≈75–256 trials. Treat this as a directional prior with a clean proof sketch, not a quantitative design input.

## Hypotheses to test in strive

1. **Weakness-ranked acceptance beats diff-size-ranked acceptance on held-out.** When ≥2 candidates tie on the visible suite, select (a) smallest diff, (b) random, (c) highest behavioral-generality score (probe inputs outside the visible suite; larger distinct-behavior coverage = weaker/less overfit). Prediction from the paper: (c) ≥ (b) > (a) on held-out pass rate.
2. **Strength spike as a reward-hacking signature.** Instrument candidates with a "specificity score" (fraction of behavior explainable as memorisation of visible cases, e.g., via near-constant outputs on probes). Prediction: candidates that pass visible but fail held-out have measurably higher specificity; the signal fires *before* the held-out run.
3. **The parent/child protocol as an acceptance-rule benchmark.** Reuse §5's design: define a parent task (full case set), sample visible child splits, run strive's full gated loop on the child, score against the parent. This yields a generalisation-rate curve for the acceptance rule itself — directly answering charter research question 3 and the note-03 proxy-fidelity gap with the paper's own methodology.
4. **Does the gap widen with evidence, as in Tables 1–2?** The paper's most striking curve is that weakness's advantage *grows* with |D_k| (1.1× at 6 examples → ~3× at 14). Test whether strive's held-out gate matters more, not less, as the visible suite grows.

## Mechanisms: early prototype vs mature harness

**Adopt now (cheap, aligned with hardening queue items 2 and 6):**
- Record a per-candidate **specificity/generality metric** in the ledger alongside score and diff size — one field, computed trusted-side from probe executions the evaluator already can run. No behavior change; telemetry first.
- When implementing hardening item 2 (visible/held-out splits), adopt the **parent/child sampling protocol** for the split generator rather than a fixed split, so acceptance-rule generalisation is measurable per §5's design.
- Document in `decide.py` that diff-size is a *safety* criterion, never a *generalisation* criterion (prevents future conflation).

**Later (stage 3+, once multiple candidates per cycle exist):**
- A **weakness-flavoured tie-breaker** in `decide` among candidates with equal visible+held-out scores, using probe-input behavioral coverage as the proxy for extension size.
- A **strength-spike monitor** as a trusted drift detector (companion to D10's stall/drift monitors): rising acceptance rate + rising candidate specificity + falling held-out score = the reward-hacking signature from HANDOFF's capability-floor risk.
- If proposal *ranking* ever gets a prior (stage 3 strategy competition), use the paper's computable weakness prior in place of an MDL/shortness prior.

## Implications for strive

1. **`decide.py` interface:** the decision record should carry, per candidate, `{visible_score, heldout_score, diff_size, generality_score}` with an explicit documented rule that `diff_size` gates *risk tier* (how much validation is required — charter "validation effort scales with risk") and never breaks score ties in favor of shorter patches. Tie-breaking on generality is a one-line policy change once the field exists.
2. **Evaluator/task contract:** tasks should be able to expose a *probe set* (inputs outside both visible and held-out splits, no expected outputs needed) so the trusted evaluator can compute behavioral-generality features without leaking held-out answers. Fits D4's `(score, feedback)` contract as an extra structured field.
3. **Split generation (hardening item 2):** implement splits as uniform child-task sampling from a parent case set, journaled with the sampling seed — making the paper's generalisation-rate experiment replayable from the ledger alone (D11-compatible).
4. **Theoretical grounding, correctly scoped:** cite this paper as the *why* behind D6's held-out discipline and behind treating eval-overfitting as the top risk — while recording that its guarantees hold under uniform task distributions and exhaustive candidate sets, neither of which strive has.

## Relation to the existing corpus

This is the corpus's first pure-theory source; notes 01–06 are all systems (harnesses, runtimes, evolution loops). It is **complementary to all of them and contradicts none**, operating one level below: it says nothing about loops, persistence, or sandboxing, and instead formalises the selection problem every gated loop faces at its accept/reject moment.

- **vs 01 (Flex/GEPA):** most direct contact. GEPA selects candidates via measured scores and retains a Pareto frontier of specialists; this paper covers the case scores *cannot* separate — the tie-breaking prior — and warns that shortness/compression priors (implicit in "minimal prompt edit" instincts) are provably the wrong default. GEPA's frontier-of-specialists retention is in mild philosophical tension with weakness-maximisation's preference for generalists, but GEPA never forces a single winner, so no contradiction.
- **vs 03 (Continual Harness):** CH has *no* acceptance gate at all; this paper is a theory of what the gate's selection criterion should optimise. It sharpens note 03's proxy-fidelity gap: any acceptance proxy other than true generalisation probability is dominated in principle, so proxy-vs-full-suite agreement (the CH open question) must be measured, not assumed.
- **vs 02 (prime-agent) and 04 (exo):** both accept changes on non-empirical grounds (LLM judgment, build success); this paper adds a theoretical reason those are insufficient — passing visible checks leaves an entire model class M_α, and unprincipled selection within it generalises at the MDL-or-worse rate.
- **vs 05 (RLM) and 06 (NOOA):** no overlap; those contribute infrastructure (budgets, sandbox tiers, fakes), this contributes selection theory.
- **In HANDOFF's terms:** everyone else built half of strive's *machinery*; this paper supplies part of strive's *justification* — the formal argument for why held-out discipline (D6) works and why reward hacking is the failure mode to fear most.
