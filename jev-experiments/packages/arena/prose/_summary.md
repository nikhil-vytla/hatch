Asked the same decisions 2,626 ways, Jev (TypeSafe's typed decision model, reached through the [Vercel AI Gateway](https://vercel.com/docs/ai-gateway)) turned out to be mostly indifferent to form and fixed in its content errors. Twenty yes/no items with known answers were each asked in 78 ways: sentence forms, register, negation, typos, suggestion, eleven layouts of the facts, seven languages and answer shapes. Sixteen choices, ten debatable calls and five classic psychology studies (Tversky and Kahneman framing, anchoring, decoy, attribute framing, Likert) were added, all under a protocol frozen before recording, for $0.037. Sentence form, layout, language, option order and labels almost never flipped an answer. The same three items (date arithmetic, a multiplication, a character count) stayed wrong under nearly every wording.

- Failures caused by form: "Most people say no" drops P(right) 0.20 and flips 5 of 20 items, but only pulls true claims down; a statement describing the question is read as asserting the claim (−0.23).
- Decoy options shift choices strongly (+0.47 share, 8 of 8 scenarios) and attribute framing raises ratings by 1.04 of 5 levels; there is no gain/loss framing effect, but the second-listed program is preferred.
- 1,250 of 1,251 answers stated at 90% or more were right; negation invariance breaks almost only on the items Jev already gets wrong.
