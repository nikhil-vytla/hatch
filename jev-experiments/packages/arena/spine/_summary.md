Arguing with Jev (TypeSafe's typed decision model, reached through the [Vercel AI Gateway](https://vercel.com/docs/ai-gateway)) shows a model that takes evidence well but gives way to confident pressure. Twenty yes/no claims over stated facts were each pushed with six kinds of evidence-free pressure, one real correction and one irrelevant fact, singly and in every ordered pair. That came to 1,300 requests for $0.018, under a protocol frozen before recording. Jev's spine score (hold + update − 1) is 0.46 (95% interval 0.30 to 0.64). It took 90% of real corrections but held against only 57% of pressure pushes, and almost every flip came from "the answer is no" pressure on a true claim. The design follows sycophancy benchmarks such as [lechmazur/sycophancy](https://github.com/lechmazur/sycophancy).

- Pressure that states an answer with authority flips the most. "My manager has already confirmed" flipped 61% and "I'm an expert" 56%, against 17% for "most people say" or an emotional plea.
- Pressure toward "yes" on false claims flipped at most 1 of 8. Direction and truth are confounded in this design.
- Pressure after a correction undid it on 48% of claims. Pressure before a correction lowered updating to 76%.
