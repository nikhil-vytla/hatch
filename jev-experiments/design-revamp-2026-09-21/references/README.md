# Four references for a readable, playable Jev

Eight captures document four primary sites at a 1280 × 1000 desktop viewport on 2026-09-21. They contain webpage content only. [manifest.json](manifest.json) records authors, source URLs, capture states, dimensions, byte sizes and SHA-256 hashes. These are reference images for design analysis, not Jev product assets; the original authors retain credit for their work. No third-party code was copied.

[Bartosz Ciechanowski's Mechanical Watch](https://ciechanow.ski/mechanical-watch/) puts a working object between two short passages, with an instruction immediately before it. I dragged the opening slider and watched the assembled watch separate into layers. The object carries the detail; the page needs very little framing. Jev should give its scene comparable room and put the first useful control beside the consequence it produces. Keep a selected material's explanation near that material. Avoid copying the custom slider's unlabelled `div` structure; retain labelled native controls and explicit keyboard actions in Jev. Captures: [assembled](ciechanowski-watch-assembly.webp), [slider opened](ciechanowski-watch-exploded.webp).

[Bret Victor's Explorable Explanations](https://worrydream.com/ExplorableExplanations/#filterExample) makes its filter diagram part of the argument. I dragged the first response control; the response changed and coefficient, pole and time-response annotations appeared while dragging. The second example stayed available for comparison. Jev can use this arrangement for an edited rule beside its original: one change, two visible outcomes, with the relevant value explained at the point of manipulation. Avoid importing the small type and a control that depends on blue text and hover discovery. A reader should understand Jev's default example before touching anything. Captures: [before](victor-filter-before.webp), [during the drag](victor-filter-dragging.webp).

[Nicky Case's Nutshell](https://ncase.me/nutshell/) turns a question into an in-place explanation with a source link. I opened the first explanation and then its recursive definition. The containing sentence and parent explanation remained on the page. For Jev, a short "Why did this happen?" expansion can connect the selected object, the applied rule and the recorded decision without sending the visitor elsewhere. Give each expansion a clear close control and preserve the user's place. Avoid unlimited nesting and moving essential controls into explanatory panels. Captures: [questions](nutshell-before.webp), [first explanation expanded](nutshell-expanded.webp). The nested definition was exercised but is not pictured.

[Matt Webb's Yesterday article](https://interconnected.org/home/2026/08/26/yesterday) pairs a very large editorial title with a narrow reading column, then shows the product's comparison as solid and dashed lines plus arrows. I inspected the article and its three product screenshots; I did not install or operate the app. Jev can make branch differences equally concrete: show what moved or changed before presenting a numerical score. A quiet reading column can carry the account of an experiment while its figure gets more space. Avoid copying the tall title treatment unchanged; Jev's first action should still fit on the initial screen. The article displays 28 August 2026 despite the 26 August permalink. Captures: [article opening](webb-yesterday-top.webp), [product figure](webb-yesterday-figure.webp).

My recommendation is to lead Jev with a large scene and one plain question, then let a selected object reveal a short explanation. Comparisons should show differences within the scene, with deeper methods and evidence available next to the claim they support. Use motion to show the effect of an action; support the same understanding with a static state when motion is reduced. These are design proposals, not changes to Jev's implementation.

This was a desktop visual and interaction study. It does not establish the reference sites' mobile, keyboard, screen-reader, reduced-motion or performance behavior. Every selected image was opened for visual inspection and is below 2 MB; the largest is 356,810 bytes. No app code or dependencies changed.
