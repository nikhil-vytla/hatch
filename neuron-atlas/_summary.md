Every MLP neuron of five open models (GPT-2, SmolLM2-135M, Qwen3-0.6B on CPU; Qwen3.5-4B and Qwen3-8B on molab's free RTX PRO 6000 GPUs, driven through marimo pair) was recorded: top documents with per-token activations, the logit lens of each output direction, and replies with neurons pushed up or down. For Qwen3.5-4B, every one of its 294,912 neurons was pushed on "Who are you?", "Tell me a short story." and "Who am I?" (1.77M replies), labeled by DeepSeek-flash on a filtered subset plus a 5% random sample so the counts estimate all replies. Everything is parquet on the Hub as [nikhilxvytla/neuron-atlas](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nikhilxvytla/neuron-atlas), read by a static [explorer Space](https://huggingface.co/spaces/nikhilxvytla/neuron-atlas-explorer) with a persona library, overview charts and an in-browser SmolLM2 that steers with exact PyTorch parity.

- Labels pass a held-out check at 0.75–0.82 against a 0.50 null, and read-side peakiness does not pick steering-useful neurons.
- Pushing every neuron rarely makes a persona (0.5% of replies, peaking in layers 1–7); far more often it garbles the model's own name ("I am Q", "Qian"), up to ~40% of pushes in layers 20–27.
- Asked "Who am I?", 13% of pushes tell the user who they are, peaking at ~40% in layers 19–20; a sizable share is a role swap ("You are Qwen3.5, the large language model…"), and these neurons barely overlap the model-persona ones.
- What a push makes the model say follows the neuron's own logit-lens words, and replayed neuronary recipes reproduce at the authors' dose 46% of the time.
