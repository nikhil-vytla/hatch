Every MLP neuron of five open models (GPT-2, SmolLM2-135M, Qwen3-0.6B on CPU; Qwen3.5-4B and Qwen3-8B on molab's free RTX PRO 6000 GPUs, driven through marimo pair) was recorded: top documents with per-token activations, the logit lens of each output direction, and replies with single neurons pushed up or down against random controls. DeepSeek-flash labeled about 9,000 neurons, each checked on held-out documents against a shuffled-label null, and steering grew from single pushes to multi-neuron recipes in the [neuronary](https://neuronary.org/about) format and a general steering-program format (units, ops, position gates, schedules). The parquet output is on the Hub as [nikhilxvytla/neuron-atlas](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nikhilxvytla/neuron-atlas) and feeds a static explorer Space (hyparquet plus a DuckDB-WASM SQL tab), a Gradio live app and a marimo notebook.

- Labels pass the held-out check at 0.75–0.82 against a 0.50 null; the labeler's confidence predicts quality (hand audit: confidence 5 good 9/10, confidence ≤3 good 2/15).
- At every size, the most "peaked" neurons are no more persona-prone than random ones; the neurons in neuronary's hand-found Qwen3.5-4B recipes sit at the median of the peakiness ranking, so read-side peakiness does not find steering-useful neurons.
- What a push makes the model say follows the neuron: replies contain its own logit-lens words several times more often than another neuron's (Qwen3-8B's Dickens neuron at +0.5: "You may call me Scrooge").
- Replayed neuronary recipes reproduce their described effect at the authors' dose 46% of the time (36% partially).
