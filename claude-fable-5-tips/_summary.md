Anthropic's newest model, Claude Fable 5, is capable enough that the prescriptive prompting habits developed for older models start to work against it — enumerated rules, tight checkpoints, and treating the model like autocomplete all suppress capability rather than control it. Starting from a tweet by Anthropic's Thariq and Simon Willison's post "Fable's judgement," and corroborating against Anthropic's official [prompting guide](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5), the consistent recommendation is to give the model judgement and intent rather than rules, delegate mechanical implementation to cheaper subagents, and explicitly ground its self-reported progress in tool-call evidence. Two reusable artifacts came out of this: a drop-in AGENTS.md addendum encoding these patterns, and a small skill implementing Anthropic's recommended fresh-context verifier-subagent pattern for long autonomous runs.

- Judgement over rules: replace enumerated instructions with a judgement call plus the reasoning behind it.
- Delegate: use Fable for architecture/review/debugging, push implementation to Sonnet/Haiku subagents, prefer async delegation.
- Ground progress claims in actual tool results — this nearly eliminated fabricated status reports in Anthropic's own testing.
- Avoid two anti-patterns: asking the model to echo its reasoning as user-facing text (triggers a safety refusal) and showing a live context-remaining countdown (triggers premature session-ending suggestions).
