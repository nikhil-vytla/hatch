A data layer for RL rollout sandboxes turns out to be a market-shaped design problem as much as a systems one: the buyers are RL environment companies with heterogeneous runtimes, so the winning point in the design space must meet them at a mount, not replace their fleet. The exploration enumerates seven axes across 14,400 nominal combinations, prunes with constraints derived from seven jobs, and lands on a checkpoint-native rollout store: lazy delivery of unmodified container images, copy-on-write diff capture at rollout stage boundaries, branchable snapshot trees, and the customer's own object storage as source of truth. A working prototype then grounds the delivery half with no container runtime and no paid platform: it pulls an OCI image straight from the registry API, traces five stand-in agent tasks in a chroot under strace, joins accesses to layer ownership, and repacks by observed access, replicating the [Slacker (USENIX FAST '16)](https://www.usenix.org/conference/fast16/technical-sessions/presentation/harter) result on a modern environment-shaped image (tasks touch 0.3-17% of files, 5-39% of bytes) and cutting up-front delivery 3.2x with an access-based hot tier. A companion prototype turns dragged-in files plus a one-line description into a content-addressed task bundle with a deterministic seed script and grader contract, deduplicating shared seeds across bundles.

- Build-order layers are the wrong delivery unit: the dependency layer was 0% relevant to three of five tasks and 43-62% to the other two; access-based tiers dominate.
- Profile-guided prefetch is a floor, not a ceiling: leave-one-out coverage hits 87-100% for API-shaped tasks but 23% for filesystem-trawling ones, arguing for hot-tier prefetch plus a lazy tail.
- Re-grading 10,000 stored checkpoints costs about $19 of compute versus roughly $456 plus all agent-loop model tokens to re-run.
