Auditing three Parallax design choices against their upstream sources settled what is replication and what is our own invention. The [microsoft/evolving-intent](https://github.com/microsoft/evolving-intent) evaluation at pinned commit 993d6be runs two conditions, single-turn against evolved, over a shared task-ID subset, and compares aggregate mean accuracies with no pairing and no statistics; its only turn-matched control sits in a post-hoc appendix. Parallax's three-arm paired design is therefore an addition, and the cheap two-condition screen should reproduce the paper's delta first. Upstream turn delivery is harness-side and unskippable: the eval loop intercepts the agent's submission and force-injects the next scripted turn, grading through the official SWE-bench `run_evaluation` harness. That made Parallax's agent-callable `advance()` tool an undeclared deviation, since an agent could grade itself before the intervention arrived. On QC, the field splits cleanly between executable admission gates and checklist-driven human review, which is why Parallax admission ended up as code plus a Cursor skill.

- Upstream published scripts do not budget-match arms: the SWE evolve condition gets 200 steps per turn over 7 turns against 100 total for single-turn.
- No statistical test code exists in either upstream repo. Every published comparison is a table of means or a descriptive percentage.
- Prime Intellect's paired no-op and gold validation with flakiness retries is the strongest admission QC found, and it adapted to Parallax directly.
- The `advance()` finding was acted on: turn delivery moved harness-side in [`../swebench-experiment-prerequisites-20260803/`](../swebench-experiment-prerequisites-20260803/README.md), and the agent no longer has a turn-control tool.
