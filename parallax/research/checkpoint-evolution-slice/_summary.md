Checkpoint evolution, Parallax's second synthesis strategy, went from design to executed paid screening in this unit. An agent extends its own persisting workspace across an ordered checkpoint sequence while sealed test obligations accumulate, following the mechanics of [SlopCodeBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24755), with strict frozen Pydantic models, digest-chained receipts, container-sandboxed verification, and validators that make an ungated episode unrepresentable. The preregistered 60-call Haiku screening ran unchanged for $0.28 against a $5 cap and separated the arms completely at stage 3: carry-reference verified strict on 10/10 seeds while the evolved arm, re-serializing its own increasingly verbose workspace, overran the family's 4096-byte cap on 10/10. That separation happened at the budget boundary, before any test ran, so it is not evidence about verification decay, and a follow-up run varying the cap and the reply format is what will settle it ([PR #27](https://github.com/nikhil-vytla/hatch/pull/27)).

- 197 offline tests plus a committed 24-mutant behavioral gauntlet, all killed, covering obligation accumulation, sandbox lockdown, metering, and spend approval.
- Every model-written case executed inside a digest-pinned container with no network and a read-only rootfs. The live path has no host-execution fallback.
- The evolved arm cost 1.6 times the carry arm on identical schedules, because accumulated verbosity comes back as billed input tokens. That cost is the run's one unambiguous result.
