Checkpoint evolution — Parallax's second synthesis strategy, following the mechanics of [SlopCodeBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24755) — went from design through two preregistered paid screenings in this unit, and the second one reversed the first's headline. The initial 60-call Haiku run produced a complete stage-3 arm separation (carry-reference strict 10/10, evolved 10/10 budget failures), but that result was confounded: the evolved arm is the only arm that must re-serialize its own accumulated workspace inside the shared flat 4096-byte cap, making the arms nominally budget-matched but effectively unmatched. A disambiguation run that changed exactly one lever (escalating caps 4096/8192/12288, output tokens 4096) erased the separation entirely — evolved strict 10/10 everywhere, paired bounds [0, 0] — so the original finding was the instrument, not self-accumulation. What survives is a cost/bloat signature (evolved workspaces 2.3× larger, 1.52× the spend on identical schedules) and a structural fix: `budget_headroom_violations` now refuses budget-confounded designs on the live path before any money moves ([PR #27](https://github.com/nikhil-vytla/hatch/pull/27)).

- Total metered spend $0.56 across 120 stage calls; 120/120 delivered validated, digest-chained evidence through the pinned container sandbox with zero infrastructure failures.
- 216 offline tests plus a 25-mutant behavioral gauntlet (all killed), including the new budget-headroom refusal mutant.
- The nominal-vs-effective budget-matching constraint is written into the method doc for future families: whatever an arm must re-emit as a function of carried state, the cap schedule must grow at least as fast as the legal carried state.
