Parallax is a research harness that turns observed agent failure modes into controlled experiments, with two synthesis strategies implemented: [Evolving Intent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20734v1) on GSM8K and SWE-bench Verified, and checkpoint evolution on a hand-authored checkpoint family. Everything runs through strict frozen Pydantic models, sealed verifiers, canonical evidence, and source-clustered reporting. The SWE path pins the paper's published source IDs, holds the gold patch under sealed authority, gives every arm one equal episode budget, delivers scripted turns from the evaluation loop rather than an agent-callable tool, and grades candidate patches with the pinned official [SWE-bench](https://github.com/SWE-bench/SWE-bench) harness in a separate digest-pinned image. Seven real-provider runs have executed for \$16.23 metered, and one of them resolved something: a 1,296-episode live GSM8K run put evolved minus base accuracy at -0.109 with a 95% source-clustered interval of [-0.160, -0.060], then decomposed it into -0.086 from multi-turn presentation alone and -0.023 from the intent evolving on top, the second spanning zero. Most of the apparent evolving-intent penalty is the cost of using turns at all, which only a three-arm design could show. The SWE-bench contrast returned a paired delta of +0.111 with an interval spanning the entire estimand, and the checkpoint-evolution screening split its arms completely at stage 3 until a second run raised the byte cap, changed nothing else, and the separation disappeared. The cap was the effect. The honest read on all of it, retraction and spend audit included, is in `docs/FINDINGS.md`.

- GSM8K has all three arms against a real provider. SWE-bench has real Opus episodes, three source clusters, and no matched control. Checkpoint evolution has two paid runs on exactly one task family.
- Parallax follows the semantics of the immutable [Microsoft reference revision](https://github.com/microsoft/evolving-intent/tree/993d6be9597ac03854b46362ccd647eb1bfd267a) without claiming provider-text parity, generated-pool reproduction, or paper-score reproduction.
